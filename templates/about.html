Project Overview

The AI-Based Spam and Phishing Email/Message Detection System is a multi-tier cybersecurity defense application engineered to detect, classify, and explain malicious communication patterns. Modern attackers increasingly bypass traditional static blacklists using polymorphic language, newly registered domains, transliterated vernaculars, and psychological urgency tactics.

This system implements a hybrid defense model uniting statistical Machine Learning (TF-IDF with Logistic Regression), Natural Language Processing semantic trigger analysis, and zero-click structural URL forensics to produce an interpretable risk score from 0 to 100 with clear explainability.

College Viva Presentation Q&A

Spam refers to bulk, unsolicited messages typically sent for commercial promotion, counterfeit goods, lottery scams, or cheap services. While annoying and deceptive, spam rarely aims to steal user credentials directly.

Phishing is a targeted cyber attack designed to trick victims into revealing sensitive credentials, passwords, OTPs, or financial information by impersonating trusted entities (banks, PayPal, IT support). Phishing employs artificial urgency, fear, and deceptive spoofed links.

TF-IDF evaluates the relative importance of a word in a document relative to an entire corpus. Term Frequency (TF) measures how frequently a token appears in the specific text. Inverse Document Frequency (IDF) reduces the weight of ubiquitous tokens (like "the", "and") that appear across all messages, allowing distinctive terms like "verify", "winner", or "suspended" to have higher weight.

Automated model retraining without human verification makes machine learning systems vulnerable to Adversarial Data Poisoning Attacks. An attacker could intentionally submit malicious emails labeled as "Safe" until the model learns to ignore dangerous indicators. Staging feedback for human supervisor auditing maintains model integrity.

The URL analyzer normalizes character substitutions (leetspeak: 0 → o, 1 → l, @ → a) and compares root domains against a protected dictionary of high-value brands (PayPal, Chase, Apple, Microsoft, SBI, HDFC). If a brand name appears inside a domain that does not match the official authorized root domain (e.g. paypa1-update.com or apple.verify-session.xyz), it is flagged as spoofed.
Risk Scoring Rubric
Low Risk (0 – 30)
Normal correspondence, transactional receipts, routine notifications without deceptive links or intimidation.
Medium Risk (31 – 60)
Commercial promotions, unsolicited bulk marketing, prize announcements, discount claims without direct credential theft.
High Risk (61 – 100)
Account suspension threats, urgent credential verification, password reset links, brand lookalikes, or raw numeric IP URLs.
